TASK:
Update Meridian’s insight generation prompt and language guardrails so action steps are concrete and user-friendly.

CURRENT ISSUE:
Some generated recommendations are safe but vague.

Example:
“Keep protein moderate.”

This leaves the user wondering:
- Am I eating too much protein?
- Am I eating too little protein?
- What does moderate mean?
- What exactly do I do today?

Meridian should not leave the user guessing.

PRODUCT VOICE:
Meridian should sound:
- simple
- calm
- kind
- clear
- practical
- non-alarming
- non-diagnostic
- non-prescriptive
- easy for a non-medical user to understand

CORE RULE:
Every action step must pass the Mom Test:

“If the user has to ask ‘how?’ or ‘how much?’, the action step failed.”

REQUIREMENTS:

1. Remove “mechanistic” from optimization tone preset

Find the tone preset for:

optimizacion

Current language may say something like:
“Deeper, mechanistic. Explain the why with precision.”

Remove “mechanistic.”

Replace with something like:
“Contextual and practical. Explain how the signal relates to the user’s goals using simple language and actionable adjustments.”

Do not encourage causal/mechanistic speculation.

2. Add action-step clarity rule to system prompt

Add a strong rule:

Every action step must be concrete and behavior-based.

Each action step should include at least:
- a clear verb
- the object/action
- a practical anchor when relevant:
  today, with your next meal, before bed, during your next walk, after dinner, etc.
- what to avoid if the action could be misinterpreted

Preferred formula:
[Verb] + [Object] + [Anchor]

Examples:
Good:
“Add one extra glass of water with your next meal.”
“Stick to your usual protein portions today.”
“Take a 20-minute easy walk after dinner.”
“Keep training light today; avoid high-intensity intervals.”

Bad:
“Keep protein moderate.”
“Support hydration.”
“Optimize recovery.”
“Monitor your energy.”
“Balance electrolytes.”

3. Add vague language restriction

Add a prompt rule discouraging or banning vague action language.

Do not allow action steps to use these phrases unless they are immediately paired with a specific behavior:
- moderate
- optimize
- support
- balance
- monitor

Examples:
Bad:
“Support hydration.”
Better:
“Drink water steadily through the morning and add one glass with lunch.”

Bad:
“Balance electrolytes.”
Better:
“Keep sodium and potassium intake steady by choosing normal meals today; do not add extra electrolyte supplements unless already part of your routine.”

Bad:
“Monitor symptoms.”
Better:
“Take note of dizziness, swelling, or unusual fatigue today; if it repeats or worsens, bring it to a qualified clinician.”

4. Replace nutrition/supplement wording in action template

Find the action step output schema / description where nutrition may be described as:
“specific food or supplement with timing”
or similar.

Replace it with safer language:

“Nutrition guidance — what to eat, keep steady, or avoid today. Do not recommend a specific supplement unless medication/safety checks explicitly allow it.”

Important:
Do not invite the model to name supplements casually.
Do not suggest dosages.
Do not suggest starting supplements.

5. Add preferred replacement examples

Add explicit examples in the prompt.

Protein:
Bad:
“Keep protein moderate.”

Preferred:
“Stick to your usual protein portions today.”
or
“Stick to your usual protein portions today. No need to add extra protein while hydration is today’s priority.”

Hydration:
Bad:
“Support hydration.”

Preferred:
“Drink water steadily through the day. Add one extra glass with your next meal.”

Electrolytes:
Bad:
“Balance electrolytes.”

Preferred:
“Keep sodium and potassium intake steady through normal meals today. Avoid adding new electrolyte supplements unless already part of your routine.”

Movement:
Bad:
“Optimize recovery.”

Preferred:
“Keep movement easy today. Choose a 20-minute walk instead of intense training.”

Monitoring:
Bad:
“Monitor your symptoms.”

Preferred:
“Take note of unusual dizziness, swelling, or fatigue today. If it repeats or worsens, bring it to a qualified clinician.”

6. Keep hedged, non-causal framing

Ensure the prompt still requires language like:
- may reflect
- could suggest
- may be related to
- is worth watching
- today’s priority is

Avoid:
- caused by
- this means
- your body is not clearing
- your kidneys are failing
- your liver is struggling
- you need to
- treat
- diagnose
- disease
- cure
