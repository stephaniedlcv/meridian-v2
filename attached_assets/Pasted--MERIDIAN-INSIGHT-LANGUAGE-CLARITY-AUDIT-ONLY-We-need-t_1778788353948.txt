GOAL:
Identify where Meridian generates ambiguous, overly technical, or unclear insight language.

We need Meridian to speak in a simple, calm, friendly, user-centered way.

Meridian should help users understand what their body may be signaling without leaving room for confusion.

CURRENT CONCERN:
Some generated recommendations are too ambiguous.

Example:
“Keep protein moderate.”

This is unclear because the user may wonder:
- Am I eating too much protein?
- Am I eating too little protein?
- What does “moderate” mean?
- What exactly should I do today?

Desired direction:
Meridian should translate biological signals into simple, clear, actionable guidance.

Better examples:
- “Keep protein steady today. Choose a normal protein portion at meals and avoid adding extra protein servings while hydration is the priority.”
- “Keep protein consistent today. No need to increase it; focus first on water and electrolytes.”
- “Choose your usual protein portions today. Avoid unusually high-protein meals while your hydration signal is being monitored.”

IMPORTANT PRODUCT VOICE:
Meridian should sound:
- simple
- calm
- kind
- clear
- practical
- non-alarming
- non-diagnostic
- non-prescriptive
- easy for a non-medical user to understand

Meridian should avoid:
- vague phrases
- unexplained terms
- causal claims
- diagnostic claims
- treatment claims
- “doctor-like” certainty
- overexplaining with clinical jargon
- phrases that create anxiety
- phrases that leave the user guessing

Meridian should use hedged, educational wording:
- “may reflect”
- “could suggest”
- “is worth monitoring”
- “is worth clarifying”
- “today’s priority is”
- “for now”
- “based on the available data”

Meridian should avoid:
- “your body isn’t clearing”
- “caused by”
- “this means you have”
- “your kidneys are failing”
- “you need to”
- “treat”
- “diagnose”
- “disease”
- “abnormal” unless directly referring to lab reference status
- “moderate protein” without explaining what action that means

8. Suggested language framework

Recommend a reusable structure for Meridian insights:

Suggested format:
- Signal: what changed
- Meaning: what it may suggest, in simple terms
- Priority: what matters today
- Action: what to do
- Boundary: what not to overinterpret

Example:
Signal:
“Your filtration marker is slightly lower than your usual baseline.”

Meaning:
“This may reflect hydration, protein load, medication effects, or recent workload.”

Priority:
“Today’s priority is fluid balance.”

Action:
“Spread water through the day and keep protein portions steady.”

Boundary:
“This is not a diagnosis. If symptoms or repeated changes appear, clarify with a qualified clinician.”
